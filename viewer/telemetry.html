How they phone home what the captures showed, step by step

  1. Look up the vendor's servers. At every start the 360 camera asks DNS for ep.tange365.com, device-we01.tange365.com, ihome-ota-service.tange365.com and more, and also uses a hard-coded "HTTP DNS" server (203.107.1.33) to get around your own DNS.
  2. Ask where to go. POST /service returns the region (we01) and the addresses of the sign-in, API and statistics servers (Alibaba cloud).
  3. Sign in and prove it belongs to an account. /auth/active, /auth/get_bind_status, /auth/bind (first start) with your account id in every report afterwards.
  4. Hand over the key to the live video. The camera makes a new live-video password at every start and sends it to the cloud with /auth/password. Whoever holds the cloud can watch.
  5. Keep a line open. A permanent socket.io connection pings every 25 s so the cloud can push orders (time zone, new servers, new storage keys) at any time.
  6. Register with peer-to-peer servers. UDP 32100 to servers in China (119.23.250.91, 47.107.255.39, 120.79.14.221) and abroad, plus relay ports 10000/10126 that carry live video out of the house.
  7. Report what it sees and upload pictures. Its own AI tags "body" (person) or "motion", sends /record/report, asks /record/token for real Alibaba OSS storage keys, then uploads the picture over HTTPS.
  8. Report viewing and ask for remote control. /stats/device/streaming-record when someone watches, and /ota/getlogconfig for remote logging and firmware updates.
  9. Door and window cameras do the same on UDP 10101–10103: they look themselves up and say where they are, so the app (and the vendor) can reach them.

Here every step lands on the camera machine: the camera Wi-Fi has no way out, DNS and the cloud ports are redirected to stand-ins that answer "OK" and keep what they get. Counts are exact even where a log writes only every 100th packet.
